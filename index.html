10Notes & Design Data

General Notes

  1. Existing building per PSB signed drawings: 25′×28′ out-to-out, 12′-0″ eave, 2:12 roof, ridge ±14′-1″. Steel columns at frame lines 1·2·3 (14′-0″ o.c.); one sidewall girt at 7′-6″; eave strut at 12′-0″; 26 ga PBR panels, Pearl Gray. O.H. door 16′×10′ centred in endwall line 1; 3′-4″ walkdoor in front sidewall near line 1.
  2. Ledger bolts land at the three steel columns only — panel skin, single girt and eave strut cannot carry roof load. The ledger is an LVL beam spanning 14′ column-to-column (Detail 4). The panel has vertical ribs (±1¼″ @ 12″ o.c.): a flat pan runs full-height over each column, so bolts go through the pan with a vertical rib-depth packer strip behind the LVL — bolt torque bears on the column, never on the ribs.
  3. Existing eave gutter remains — the carport roof drops to clear it: ledger top at 11′-0″ leaves ±6″ of wall panel below the gutter for a surface-mounted apron flashing, stitch-screwed and sealed to the panel (Detail 4). Gutter overflow and roof slide land on the carport roof, which drains to the new 5″ fascia gutter, downspout at the rear corner. Extend/reroute the garage downspout to discharge onto or past the carport roof. Wall water (rain, splash, condensation) runs down the vertical pans — foam closures + a drip-cap Z at the apron’s top edge force it out over the roof, and membrane over the ledger top backs it all up (Detail 4). Design for full snow plus drift and slide off the garage's 2:12 metal roof.
  4. Beam: (2)-ply 2×12 seated in notched 6×6 posts, (2) ½″Ø bolts per post; ply joints land on posts only, staggered (Detail 5). Fascia and gutter hang on the rafter tails, 12″ past the beam. 45° 2×6 knee braces at every post — face-mounted on the garage-side flush plane, butting at the ¢ of interior posts — stiffen the open row lengthwise; crosswise, the rafters tie the roof back into the steel building.
  5. Designed to OBC Div. B Part 9 (O. Reg. 332/12), with site-specific climatic loads taken from the signed PSB drawings (SB-1 — 61 psf ground snow). Applicable code sections are cited throughout the build manual.
  6. Footings 16–20″Ø × 4′-0″ minimum depth (Dysart et al frost). Setbacks & permit before digging.

2Lay out the footings2 H
PARTS 4 stakes · mason line · marking paint
TOOLS 100′ tape · hammer
  1. Mark the three column centrelines (0′ / 14′ / 28′) on the wall panel — they're your reference grid.
  2. Measure 12′-0″ out perpendicular at both ends of the wall; stretch a mason line between stakes.
  3. Mark post centres along the string: 3″ · 9′-4″ · 18′-8″ · 27′-9″.
  4. Square check: the two diagonals of the 28′ × 12′ rectangle must match within ¼″. Adjust, then paint an X at each hole centre.
CHECK — Diagonals equal within ¼″ · string 12′-0″ off the wall at both ends.
Confirm property-line setbacks in the zoning by-law before committing hole positions (Municipality of Dysart et al).
3Dig 4 holes½ DAY
PARTS nothing consumed — tarp for spoil
TOOLS one-man rental power auger w/ 16″ bit (or post-hole digger + digging bar)
  1. Do not dig until Ontario One Call clearances are in hand.
  2. Auger each hole 16″Ø to 4′-0″ minimum and into undisturbed native soil — deeper beats shallower.
  3. Clean and flatten the bottoms; no loose fill left in the hole.
  4. If the municipality wants a footing inspection, book it now with the holes open. Cover holes overnight.
CHECK — All four ≥ 4′-0″ deep · bottoms undisturbed and dry · inspection booked if required.
OBC 9.12.2.2 — minimum foundation depth below frost line · 9.15.3 — footing size & bearing. O. Reg. 332/12
4Pour + set anchors½ DAY + 48 H CURE
PARTS 4 × 16″ form tubes ×4′ · 38 bags 30 kg concrete (≥25 MPa) · 4 post-base anchor bolts
TOOLS mixing tub or wheelbarrow · hoe · torpedo level · 4′ level · gloves + dust mask
  1. Set each tube 2–3″ above grade, plumb, braced to stakes with screws.
  2. Mix stiff — about 9–10 bags per hole. Fill in lifts and rod out the air pockets.
  3. Wet-set each anchor bolt exactly on the string-line mark, plumb, threads proud enough for the base.
  4. Re-check the 9′-4″ spacing and 12′-0″ offset before the concrete stiffens — this is your last free adjustment. Cure 48 h minimum before loading.
CHECK — Anchors on layout within ⅛″ · plumb · geometry re-verified.
OBC 9.3.1 — concrete materials (use ≥25 MPa bagged mix) · 9.15 — footings & foundations. O. Reg. 332/12
5Snap the ledger line @ 11′-0″1 H
PARTS chalk
TOOLS laser level · tape · ladder
  1. Mark 11′-0″ above your slab/grade datum at each of the three columns.
  2. Walk the wall and measure the existing gutter height everywhere — the building may not be dead level. You need roughly 6″ of clear panel below the gutter for flashing; govern by the tightest spot.
  3. Snap the chalk line and confirm it's level with the laser.
CHECK — Line level within ⅛″ over 28′ · at least 4″ below the gutter everywhere.
Layout only — no code items. The 11′-0″ height exists to keep the existing gutter in service (Note 3).
6Cleat trick — hang the LVL1 H
PARTS two 2×4 offcuts · ~12 #12 stitch screws (temporary)
TOOLS impact driver · 2 ladders
  1. Screw a temporary 2×4 cleat to the wall panel ribs with its top edge 11⅞″ below the chalk line — that's the ledger depth, so the LVL's top lands on the line. The cleat is a shelf, not a support.
  2. Lift the first 14′ LVL (≈75 lb) one end at a time onto the cleat — low end first, walk the second end up.
  3. Slide it along the cleat to the column marks.
CHECK — LVL stable on the cleat · top kissing the chalk line · tight to the wall.
Temporary work — reuse or seal the stitch-screw holes when flashing at step 17.
7Drill + bolt the ledger½ DAY
PARTS 2 LVL plies 1¾″×11⅞″ ×14′ · 12 ½″Ø through-bolts + plate washers + nuts · lamination screws · vertical rib-depth packer strips (rip ±1¼″ from PT stock, or steel pipe sleeves per engineer)
TOOLS ½″ drill · auger bit (wood) · step or annular bit (steel) · ratchet + ¾″ socket · deburring tool
  1. At each column, mark four bolt positions — two rows, at least 2″ from the LVL edges, centred on the column.
  2. The panel ribs are VERTICAL @ 12″ o.c., so a flat pan runs full-height over each 8″ column — land the bolts in that pan with a packer strip between LVL and pan, so tightening bears on the column and never crushes a rib (Detail 4, top-down view; Detail 6 is the packer itself). Steel pipe sleeves around the bolts work too — even cleaner.
  3. Packer construction (Detail 6): laminate ¾″ + ½″ exterior plywood — glued and screwed — into a 1¼″ × 6″ × 11⅞″ strip (match the MEASURED rib height), one per column. Face the panel side with butyl tape / self-adhered membrane: it waterproofs the bolt penetrations and isolates the wood from the galvalume. Pre-drill ½″ holes and screw each packer to the back of the LVL at its column mark before lifting. (LVL offcut works instead of ply; never bare PT against the panel.)
  4. Drill the LVL first, then through panel + column flange from outside. Deburr the steel.
  5. Bolt with the plate washer inside the column flange. Snug — don't crush the wall panel.
  6. Hang the second ply the same way. Butt joints land at the CENTRE column only. Screw the plies together, two rows @ 12″, then final-torque all 12 bolts.
CHECK — Every bolt passes through solid steel — never panel alone · ledger top on the line · plies flush.
OBC 9.23.3 — fasteners. O. Reg. 332/12
8Bases on, measure each post1 H
PARTS 4 ABU66Z standoff bases + hardware · lumber crayon
TOOLS laser level · story pole (straight 2×4 with a tape on it)
  1. Bolt each standoff base to its anchor — the standoff keeps the post’s end grain up out of water.
  2. Set the laser to one level plane clearing all four bases. Hold the story pole on each base seat and record the reading — the four piers will NOT be identical, and this is where that difference gets absorbed.
  3. Work out each post’s cut length: seat height = ledger top − 24″ (2:12 drop over 12′) − 9⅜″ (rafter) − 11¼″ (beam) ≈ 7′-3″, measured off the REAL ledger — then adjust by each base reading. Write the length on its post and label which pier it belongs to.
CHECK — four cut lengths written on four labelled posts · arithmetic re-checked against the tallest pier.
OBC 9.17.4 — wood columns · 9.23.2 — decay protection (standoff base separates wood from concrete). O. Reg. 332/12
9Notch on the horses, stand once2–3 H
PARTS 2×4 braces + stakes · screws
TOOLS circular saw · handsaw + chisel · square · post level · impact driver · sledge
  1. On the sawhorses — where the work is easy — cut each post to its length, then lay out its notch: 3″ wide × 11¼″ deep on the face TOWARD the garage, leaving the 2½″ shoulder on the outside (Detail 5).
  2. Kerf with the circular saw from both faces, knock out the waste, pare the seat dead flat with a chisel.
  3. Stand the post on the TALLEST pier first, plumb both faces, brace two directions to stakes — then laser-check its seat before standing the rest, so any surprise costs one post, not four.
  4. Stand, plumb and brace the remaining three. Braces are load-bearing scaffolding from here — they stay until the rafters are all in (end of step 13).
CHECK — all four seats on the laser line within ⅛″ · plumb both axes · braces solid.
Bearing geometry per Detail 5 — don’t deepen the notch or thin the shoulder.
10Walk the beam plies up2 H
PARTS beam plies: 2 × 18′-8″ + 2 × 9′-4″ (from the 20′ and 10′ 2×12s) · 2×4 saddle cleats + screws
TOOLS 2 ladders · 4 clamps · impact driver
  1. Screw temporary saddle cleats across each post pair about 8″ below the seats.
  2. Carry one ply in, lean an end on the low saddle, and walk the other end up the ladder — never a full ply overhead, one end always supported.
  3. Slide the ends into the notches. Long piece first; its butt joint lands dead-centre on its post seat. Clamp.
  4. Second layer the same, joint on the OTHER interior post so the joints are staggered (Detail 5 plan view).
CHECK — Every ply end fully bearing on a shoulder · joints centred on posts · nothing spliced mid-span.
OBC 9.23.4 — beams & spans (sized for the 61 psf snow zone). O. Reg. 332/12
11Bolt the beam + knee braces2 H
PARTS 8 ½″Ø ×8″ HDG bolts + washers + nuts · lamination screws · 6 2×6 knee braces + SDS screws
TOOLS ½″ auger bit · ratchet · clamps · string line
  1. Drill two ½″ holes per post through shoulder + both plies — one each side of any joint, so both ply ends are clamped.
  2. Bolt snug, washers both sides.
  3. Screw-laminate the plies two rows @ 16″ between posts.
  4. String the beam end-to-end, pull it straight, then final torque. Remove the saddles; keep the temporary post braces.
  5. Knee braces (Detail 7): the notch leaves the beam’s GARAGE-SIDE face flush with the post’s — so the braces lie FLAT across both on the inside, out of the weather and clear of the downspout at the rear post. (4) SDS ¼″×3½″ screws per end through the brace’s flat, in shear; ends cut 45° so they sit plumb along the post and level along the beam top. END POSTS (one brace): slide it until the plumb edge is flush with the post’s outside edge and the level edge flush with the beam top — the edges are the jig. INTERIOR POSTS (two braces): the pair BUTTS at the post centreline, a symmetric V, no overlap. 6 total; these stop the row racking along the beam — crosswise, the rafters brace everything back to the building.
CHECK — Beam straight within ¼″ over 28′ · two torqued bolts per post.
OBC 9.23.3 — bolted wood connections. O. Reg. 332/12
12One template rafter → ×221 H + CUTTING
PARTS 22 2×10 ×14′ (one becomes the template)
TOOLS speed square (use the 2 mark for 2:12) · circular saw · tape · lumber crayon
  1. Cut one 2×10: plumb cut at the ledger end (hanger seat), 13′-0″ horizontal run, plumb tail cut.
  2. Test-fit in a hanger at the ledger AND on the beam — full seat contact at both. Adjust until it drops in clean.
  3. Try it at three different positions along the ledger (buildings wander); if it fits all three, mark it TEMPLATE.
  4. Trace and cut the other 21. Mark every crown and keep crowns UP.
CHECK — Template fits at three positions · 21 copies cut, crowns marked.
OBC 9.23.4.2 — rafter spans. O. Reg. 332/12
13Hangers on, rafters in½ DAY
PARTS 22 LSSR210Z sloped hangers + their specified nails · 22 H2.5AZ ties + fasteners
TOOLS hammer · tape · marker · 2 ladders
  1. Lay out 16″ o.c. along the ledger starting from the DS (rear) end.
  2. Nail every hanger at its mark — EVERY hole gets the specified hanger nail. No drywall screws, not ever, not temporarily.
  3. Drop each rafter into its hanger, nail off, and seat it on the beam — crown up.
  4. Tie every rafter to the beam with an H2.5. When all 22 are in, the frame is self-bracing — the post braces can come off.
CHECK — Every hanger hole filled with the specified fastener · crowns up · tails 12″ past the beam centreline.
OBC 9.23.3.1 — fastening schedule; hangers installed per their CCMC evaluation listing. O. Reg. 332/12

15Purlins — the roof strapping2 H
PARTS 14 2×4 ×14′ SPF · #10 ×3″ screws — 2 per crossing
TOOLS impact driver · chalk line · tape
  1. Chalk purlin lines up the slope: first one 2″ from the fascia, then 24″ o.c., last within 6″ of the wall.
  2. Lay the 2×4s FLAT across the rafters; butt joints on rafter centres, staggered row to row.
  3. Two screws at every rafter crossing. Work off ladders and planks — purlins on 24″ centres are not a floor.

16Panels — calm day only½–1 DAY
PARTS 10 galvalume panels 36″ ×14′ · 500 #10 roof screws w/ sealing washers · butyl tape · foam closures
TOOLS impact + hex driver · shears or nibbler (never an abrasive disc — it burns the coating) · gloves
  1. Wait for a dry, windless day. A 14′ panel is a sail; this is the one step where weather is a hard gate.
  2. Square the first panel at the DS (rear) end: 2″ overhang into the gutter line, rib edge parallel to the rake.
  3. Screw the flats at every purlin per the panel maker's pattern — snug the washers, don't dimple the steel.
  4. Butyl tape in every side lap plus stitch screws @ 12″. Work panel by panel toward the garage-door end.
  5. Rip the last panel to ±12″ with shears; the cut edge hides under the rake trim.
CHECK — Rib lines parallel to the rake · washers snug not crushed · even 2″ gutter overhang.
OBC 9.26.3 — minimum roof slopes (buy a panel rated for 2:12 with sealed side laps) · installed per manufacturer's instructions. O. Reg. 332/12
17Rake trim + apron flashing2 H
PARTS 4 rake trims · 4 apron flashings · 3 drip-cap Z trims (all white) · foam closures · S.A. membrane · stitch screws · butyl + sealant
TOOLS snips · impact driver · caulk gun · ladder
  1. Rake trim down both gable ends, over the panel edges.
  2. First, the backup layer: self-adhered membrane over the ledger’s top face, lapped 2″ up the wall panel — anything that ever gets behind the flashing sheds off this instead of soaking the LVL.
  3. Apron flashing: fit profile-matched foam closures in EVERY pan along the top-edge line, bedded in butyl both sides — the pans are the wall’s drainage channels and the closures force that water out over the flashing. Then bed the apron’s top edge and stitch-screw to the ribs @ 12″ o.c., at least 3″ below the gutter (which stays untouched).
  4. Cap the apron’s top edge with a drip-cap Z trim, sealed along its top only — two-stage protection, so no caulk joint takes direct rain, splash or condensation running down the wall. The cap is a plain straight trim — its leg rides the rib crowns, foam closures fill each pan behind it, and the stitch screws land in the crowns only (both views in Detail 4). Any metal-roofing supplier bends it to order, or fold your own from aluminum coil stock over a board edge.
  5. The apron's lower leg laps the roof panels ≥4″, fastened on rib crowns, foam closures underneath.
  6. Pull the step-6 cleats and land flashing screws in those holes — or seal them.
CHECK — Water path is wall → apron → panel → gutter with no reverse laps anywhere.
OBC 9.26.4 — flashing at roof–wall intersections. O. Reg. 332/12
18Gutter, downspout, done2–3 H
PARTS 3 5″ gutters · outlet · end caps · 20 hangers · downspout + 3 elbows + straps · gutter sealant · copper naphthenate (PT end cuts)
TOOLS hex driver · snips · string or level · caulk gun · garden hose
  1. Chalk a line on the sub-fascia falling about 1″ over the 28′ toward the rear; hang the gutter @ 24″ with the outlet at the rear corner.
  2. Seal every seam and end cap. From the outlet, use two elbows to jog the downspout back to the rear post (the gutter sits 12″ outboard of the post line), strap it to the post @ 4′ o.c., then to grade with a splash pad or extension pointing away from the footings.
  3. Extend the existing garage downspout so it discharges onto or past the new roof.
  4. Brush preservative on every PT cut end. Then the hose test: flood the roof and watch the apron line and gutter.
  5. Book the final inspection.
CHECK — Hose test passes — everything reaches the DS, nothing weeps behind the apron · final inspection booked.
